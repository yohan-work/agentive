Here's a one-page draft you can use now. I made a few assumptions to keep it short, and they're listed below the policy. There's one point you need to decide before this goes out. "Use AI as much as possible" and "nothing confidential ever leaves" only work together if you give people **approved tools** that are allowed to handle confidential data. Without them, the only way to keep the second promise is to restrict AI to public information, and that works against the first goal.

---

# AI Usage Policy

**Owner:** [Name/Role] · **Effective:** [Date] · **Review:** Every 6 months

**1. Purpose**
We want everyone to use AI tools every day to work faster and better. This policy explains how to do that without exposing confidential information.

**2. Scope**
This covers all employees, contractors, and interns, and any AI tool used for company work. That includes chatbots, coding assistants, meeting transcribers, browser extensions, and AI features built into other software.

**3. Approved vs. unapproved tools**
- **Approved tools** ([list, e.g. Company ChatGPT Enterprise / Claude for Work / Copilot Business]) are under company contracts. Those contracts bar vendors from training on our data and let us control retention. **Confidential data is allowed in approved tools only.**
- **Everything else** (free or personal accounts, new apps, browser plug-ins) counts as outside the company. **Only use public information in these tools.**
- The current list is at [link]. To request a new tool, contact [IT/Security]. Use of unlisted tools isn't allowed until they're approved.

**4. What never goes into an unapproved tool**
- Customer, employee, or personal data
- Source code, credentials, API keys, and security details
- Financials, pricing, contracts, and unreleased product or strategy information
- Anything labeled Confidential or Internal, or covered by an NDA

If you're not sure, treat it as confidential.

**5. Using AI output**
- **You own the result.** Check facts, numbers, code, and citations before you rely on them or share them.
- Get human review before AI-generated content goes to customers or the public, or is used in legal, HR, or financial decisions.
- Don't use AI to make final decisions about people (hiring, performance, termination).
- Follow copyright and licensing rules, and don't present AI output as someone else's work.

**6. Meetings and recordings**
Only use approved AI note-takers, and tell participants when one is running. Don't use them in calls with external parties unless those parties agree.

**7. Incidents**
If confidential information goes into an unapproved tool, report it to [Security contact] **within 24 hours**. Reporting it quickly won't get you in trouble. Hiding it will.

**8. Enforcement**
Violations may lead to disciplinary action. We'll update this policy as tools and laws change.

**Assumptions I made**
- You have (or will get) at least one enterprise AI tool with no-training and data-retention terms. If you don't, section 3 collapses to "public information only," and most of the productivity benefit goes with it.
- You already have a data classification scheme (Confidential/Internal/Public). If not, section 4 is your working definition.
- No industry-specific rules apply (e.g., HIPAA, financial services, government contracts). If they do, you'll need a short addendum.

**Risks to be aware of**
- **"Never" isn't fully enforceable through policy alone.** People will still paste things into free tools. You can reduce that with technical controls such as SSO-only access, blocking unapproved AI sites, and DLP. Written rules won't prevent it on their own.
- **Approved doesn't mean data never leaves.** Data sent to an enterprise vendor still goes to that vendor. You're relying on the contract, so have Legal/Security check the retention, training, and sub-processor terms.
- **"One page, covers everything" is a trade-off.** This page covers the rules. Detailed items like the tool list, the request process, and regulatory specifics should live in linked documents.
