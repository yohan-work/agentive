# AI Usage Policy

**Effective:** [date] · **Owner:** [role/team] · **Applies to:** all employees, contractors, and temporary staff

## 1. Purpose
We want you to use AI tools often. Use them to write, code, research, analyze, and summarize faster. This policy sets the few rules you need to do that without exposing confidential information.

## 2. Approved vs. public tools
- **Approved tools** are the ones on the [Approved AI Tools list / link]. We hold enterprise agreements for these tools that stop vendors from training on our data or keeping it. You may use them with internal and confidential information, **except** for the restricted data in Section 3.
- **Public tools** are everything else, including free or personal accounts of approved products. Use them **only with public information.**
- If you're not sure whether a tool is approved, treat it as public. To request a new tool, contact [IT/Security contact].

## 3. Data rules
| Data type | Examples | Public tools | Approved tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public | Published marketing, public docs | Yes | Yes |
| Internal / Confidential | Strategy, financials, source code, internal docs, customer names | **Never** | Yes |
| Restricted | Passwords, API keys, credentials, customer personal data, health/payment data, data under NDA or legal hold | **Never** | **Only with written approval from [Security/Legal]** |

When in doubt, treat the data as confidential.

## 4. Your responsibilities
- **You own the output.** AI makes mistakes. Before you share or ship anything it produces, check facts, code, numbers, and citations.
- **Review before it goes outside.** A person must review any AI output that reaches customers, the public, or regulators before it's sent.
- **Respect IP.** Don't use AI to copy third-party copyrighted material or reproduce it word for word. [Engineering: follow the code-scanning/licensing process for AI-generated code.]
- **No high-stakes decisions by AI alone.** Hiring, firing, performance, credit, legal, or medical decisions need a person to make them and be accountable for them.
- **Be transparent** if someone asks whether AI was used, or if [client contracts/regulations] require you to disclose it.

## 5. Never
- Paste confidential or restricted data into a public tool, a browser extension, or a personal account.
- Connect an AI tool to company systems (email, drive, repositories, CRM) unless IT has approved it.
- Use AI to deceive, impersonate, harass, or break the law or company policy.

## 6. Mistakes and questions
If you shared something you shouldn't have, report it to [security contact/channel] **right away**. If you report it quickly and honestly, we'll treat it as a learning moment, not a disciplinary one. Hiding a mistake is the serious offense. Send questions to [contact/channel].

Deliberate or repeated violations may lead to disciplinary action. We'll review this policy every [6 months] as tools change.

---

**Before you roll it out:**
1. **Publish the approved-tools list.** The policy only works if there is an easy, approved option. If there isn't one, people will fall back on public tools.
2. **Confirm your enterprise agreements** actually include no-training and data-retention terms before you list a tool as approved.
3. **Check with Legal** about any sector rules that apply to you (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA, financial regulations, client contract clauses), because they may move more data into "Restricted."
