Here's a one-page draft. One note first: "use AI as much as possible" and "nothing confidential ever leaves the company" only fit together if you have **company-approved AI tools** whose contracts keep your data from being retained or used for training. Without those, the only way to keep the second promise is to keep people from putting any real work into AI, which undercuts the first. The draft below assumes you have (or will get) at least one approved tool. Fill in the bracketed parts.

---

# [Company] AI Usage Policy

**Effective:** [date] · **Owner:** [name/team] · **Applies to:** all employees, contractors, and interns

## 1. Our stance
We want you to use AI to work faster and better: drafting, summarizing, coding, research, analysis, brainstorming. Use it every day. This policy exists so you can use it confidently without putting the company, our customers, or you at risk.

## 2. Approved tools
- **Approved for company data:** [Tool A (enterprise plan), Tool B]. These are under contracts that bar training on our data and limit retention.
- **Everything else** (personal accounts, free tiers, browser extensions, new apps) counts as **public**. Only public information goes into these tools.
- Want a new tool? Ask [IT/Security at email/channel]. We aim to reply within [5] business days.

## 3. What data can go where

| Data type | Examples | Approved tools | Other tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Published website copy, public docs | Yes | Yes |
| **Internal** | Internal processes, general non-sensitive work | Yes | No |
| **Confidential** | Customer data, personal data (PII), financials, source code, contracts, strategy, unreleased products, HR matters | Yes, if the tool is approved for this category | **Never** |
| **Restricted** | Passwords, API keys, credentials, health or payment card data, anything under legal hold or NDA that forbids it | **Never** | **Never** |

If you're not sure, treat it as confidential and ask.

## 4. Your responsibilities
- **You own the output.** Check AI output for accuracy before you use it. AI makes up facts, citations, and code.
- **Review before you ship.** AI-generated code goes through the normal review and security checks. Customer-facing content gets a human read.
- **Be transparent** when it matters: tell customers when they are talking to an AI, and tell reviewers when work is substantially AI-generated.
- **Respect IP.** Don't ask AI to copy copyrighted work or a competitor's material. Don't upload third-party content we have no right to share.
- **No high-stakes decisions by AI alone.** Hiring, firing, performance, credit, legal, or medical decisions about people need a human decision-maker.
- **No AI connected to company systems** (agents, integrations, auto-send, meeting recorders) without approval from [IT/Security].

## 5. If something goes wrong
If you put confidential or restricted data into an unapproved tool, or you spot a harmful AI output, report it to [security@ / channel] **right away**. Reporting quickly and in good faith will not be punished. Hiding it can be.

## 6. Enforcement and updates
Breaking this policy may lead to disciplinary action, up to and including termination. We review this policy every [6] months, since AI tools change fast. Questions go to [owner/channel].

---

**Before rollout, you should:**
1. Confirm which tools you actually have enterprise or no-training agreements for, and list them in Section 2. This is the piece that makes the whole policy work.
2. Line up the data categories with any classification scheme you already use.
3. Get a quick review from legal/HR if you have customer contracts, regulated data (health, finance), or staff in the EU. Those may need extra terms.
